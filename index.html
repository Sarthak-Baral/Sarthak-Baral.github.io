Decisions I made, and what they cost. I chose purpose-built sparse retrieval (BM25-style ranking) over generic embedding search, with retrieval provenance so that every supporting criterion traces to a row of a curated 984-row clinical criteria table. I made the system decline to produce a formulation when grounding is insufficient; that means it sometimes gives no answer, and the grounding-gap rate is tracked as a quality signal. I blocked, fail-closed, any model provider without a business-associate agreement. I made standards gates rather than guidance: a nine-job CI pipeline with 2,809 automated tests, import-linter architecture contracts and dependency audits, and a check that fails the build if an architecture decision record is cited but does not exist. I audit the system in dated documents (August, September and October 2026), each superseded rather than edited, with a claim-verification table that governs what is said about it externally.

Where it stands (verified 20 October 2026). Live in production with five independent psychiatrists in private practice, plus one residential facility serving ~37 users, all pilot participants. v0.5.0 shipped within three months of founding; 471 commits across 259 pull requests; automated deployment since 16 October 2026 (ADR-215); 27 of 27 live checks passed. FastAPI and PostgreSQL with three React 19 applications on AWS (RDS, S3, KMS, Secrets Manager, CloudTrail), Google OAuth with TOTP MFA, TLS 1.2/1.3 with HSTS, a multi-provider LLM gateway with OpenAI/Anthropic failover, on a HIPAA-aligned technical architecture with documented technical controls.
